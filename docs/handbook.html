01Recruiting in five minutes

Recruiting manages your whole hiring funnel inside Nextcloud. Applications become cards on a board, your team votes on them without influencing each other, interviews book themselves against real calendars, and offers need a second pair of eyes before they go out.

The pipeline

Every candidate moves through the same four active stages — New → Screening → Interview → Offer — and ends in exactly one of three final states: Hired, Rejected or Withdrawn. Each stage has its own color, used everywhere in the app: board columns, table chips and report charts. Once you know that Screening is amber and Offer is green, you can read any screen at a glance.

Who sees what

There is no separate user management. You work with the Nextcloud accounts your colleagues already have, in one of four roles:

RoleWhoWhat they can do and see
Recruiter Members of the HR group (your admin configures which group) Everything: all openings, all candidates, templates, the triage inbox, the talent pool, reports, GDPR actions
Hiring manager Picked per opening Runs the process for their opening: sees all its candidates, moves stages, assigns screeners, books interviews, creates offers, sends mail
Interviewer / screener Picked per opening or per candidate Sees only the candidates assigned to them. Reads the profile and documents, votes, joins the discussion. Never sees salary or offer data
Observer Picked per opening May watch the pipeline and reports of that opening, but not act
A Nextcloud administrator has no candidate access at all — being admin only allows configuring the mailbox and retention settings. Candidate documents never appear in anyone's Files app; they live in protected app storage.

The golden rules

  • Nothing goes out unreviewed. Every mail to a candidate shows you a preview first — you can edit every word before it is sent.
  • Every action is logged. Stage moves, votes, mails, offers — the candidate's Log tab is the complete history.
  • Votes are blind. You cannot see your colleagues' votes until you have cast your own.
  • Offers need four eyes. You can never approve your own offer.

02Finding your way around

One left sidebar, one board. That's the whole app.

The navigation

  • New opening — create a job opening (recruiters).
  • Triage inbox — applications that arrived by mail without a matching opening. The badge counts what's waiting.
  • My reviews — candidates you still owe a vote.
  • My interviews — interviews where you are on the panel.
  • Mail templates — the texts used for invitations, rejections, offers and confirmations.
  • Reports — live numbers over your openings.
  • Talent pool — candidates who agreed to be considered for future openings.
  • Refer someone — recommend a person you know for an open position. Available to every colleague, not only the hiring teams (chapter 13).
  • Openings — one entry per job, with the number of active candidates. Closed openings collapse at the bottom.
  • Handbook — this document, always one click away.

The board

Move a candidate by dragging the card to another column, or use the card's ⋮ menu if you prefer clicks (it works fully with the keyboard, too). Every move is logged and visible to the hiring team. The column header always shows the true total — if a stage holds more than 100 candidates, the column loads them in pages with a Show more button.

The board keeps itself up to date: while it is open, it checks every 30 seconds whether a colleague moved a candidate, added one or voted, and quietly reloads — your selection and an open candidate file stay where they are. If you move a card that somebody else has just moved, nothing is overwritten: you get a short note ("… was moved to Interview by someone else") and the board shows the current state.

Anatomy of a card

The time-in-stage indicator changes color as candidates wait: amber after two weeks, red after a month. Nobody should be forgotten in a column.

The toolbar

narrows the board to matching names or email addresses.  shows only candidates where something is stuck (more in chapter 9).  adds a candidate manually — with drag-and-drop CV upload.

Below the board, three chips summarize the finished candidates — — click one to show those candidates as an extra column (and in the table view); click it again, or the column's arrow, to fold them away. They are only loaded when you open them, so the board stays fast on long-running openings.

The table view

Click any column header to sort (a third click restores the original order). Managers get checkboxes: select several candidates and the bulk bar appears — — to move them together or reject them in one go (chapter 8).

03How applications arrive

Most applications should reach the board without anyone lifting a finger. That's the zero-touch intake.

By email — the normal way

Your admin connects a dedicated application mailbox (for example jobs@your-company.com). The app checks it every five minutes. Every new application mail becomes a candidate: name and address are read from the sender, the mail text lands on the candidate's timeline, and attachments become documents.

Automatic mail is recognized and left alone: bounces, out-of-office replies and newsletters never turn into "candidates" and never get a confirmation. The heartbeat line in the triage inbox counts them as skipped, so you can see that nothing was lost.

Every opening has a mail tag, shown in its header:

  • Mails to jobs+senior-backend-engineer@… land directly on that opening's board, in the "New" column.
  • The same works with [senior-backend-engineer] anywhere in the subject.
  • Everything else goes to the triage inbox.
Put the tagged address into your job postings — jobs+product-designer@… on the design job ad, and applications sort themselves. An automatic confirmation of receipt can be sent per opening, so applicants immediately know their documents arrived. The confirmation also carries the link to the candidate's own page (chapter 14).

Through the application form

Don't want applicants to write emails? In the opening's ⋯ → Edit opening & team, switch on Accept applications through a public form on the web. The dialog then shows the form's address (…/apps/recruiting/apply/<mail-tag>) — link it from your career site or job ad. Applicants enter their name and email address, write a few lines, attach their CV and agree to the processing of their data; the application lands in the opening's "New" column like a mailed one. The form only works while the opening is open, and it is protected against bots and floods, so it does not need a captcha.

Through referrals

Colleagues can recommend people they know with Refer someone in the navigation. A referral arrives in the opening's "New" column with the source "Employee referral" and the colleague's note on the timeline. Chapter 13 explains what the referring colleague sees.

When candidates reply

Mails the app sends carry a personal reply address for each candidate. When Grace answers your interview invitation, her reply — attachments included — lands on her timeline and the hiring managers are notified. Replies never clutter the inbox as duplicate "applications".

The triage inbox

Open a row and use Assign to an opening in the profile tab: the application moves into that opening's "New" column and its hiring managers are notified. Spam goes into the bin via the row's ⋮ menu — permanently, documents included.

The heartbeat line shows when the mailbox was last checked. If a check fails, a warning appears here — so a broken mailbox can never silently swallow applications. A mail that cannot be processed is tried again — without creating the candidate twice; after three failed attempts (twelve when the cause is temporary, such as a storage outage) it is moved to the mailbox folder Recruiting-Failed, and the warning tells you how many are waiting there, so someone can look at them by hand. Mails larger than 30 MB go there straight away.

Adding candidates by hand

Somebody applied at a job fair or on paper? on the opening's board opens a small form — name, contact details, drag the CV in, done. The candidate starts in "New" like everyone else.

04The candidate file

Click any card and the candidate file opens on the right — five tabs that hold everything about this person.

Profile

  • Contact details are editable by managers (Edit contact details).
  • If the same person applied for this opening before (the address is compared regardless of upper and lower case), a duplicate warning links to the earlier application, and Merge into the earlier application combines the two: documents, timeline, comments, votes, interviews and offers move over, and the second record disappears. Only applications for the same opening can be merged.
  • Also applied for lists the person's applications for other openings — as far as you have access to them — with a link to each.
  • Referred by shows the colleague who recommended this candidate (chapter 13).
  • If the candidate asked for their data to be deleted on their own page, a red banner shows the date; recruiters get a Delete now button right there (chapter 12).
  • With Nextcloud Assistant installed, an AI summary of the application can be generated — always labeled, never a decision. The summary reads the application text and the CV; the candidate's email address and phone number are never sent to the AI.
  • Documents: upload more, delete. PDFs, images and text files open in the browser; Word and OpenDocument files are downloaded. Stored privately in the app — never visible in Files. If your server runs the antivirus app, every upload is scanned first and infected files are refused.
  • At the very bottom sits the red Reject … button — deliberately out of the way of everyday clicking (chapter 8).

The header menu (…)

Next to the candidate's name: Book interview, Move to … any stage, and for recruiters the GDPR actions — Export data (a ZIP with everything about this person, for data-access requests) and Delete permanently.

Log — the complete story

The Log tab is the timeline (stage moves, votes cast, every mail with its full text, interviews, offer steps) plus the team discussion. Write a comment and @mention a colleague to notify them — the conversation stays with the candidate instead of scattering across chats.

The Votes, Interviews and Offer tabs each have their own chapter — read on.

05Screening & votes

The Votes tab is designed for one thing: honest, independent opinions.

Casting your vote

Pick and add a comment. A "No" always needs a written reason — "not a fit" is not a reason. You can update your vote at any time.

Why can't I see the other votes? Because you haven't voted yet. Votes of others stay hidden until you cast your own, so your opinion is really yours. You can see who has voted (their names are listed) — just not what they said. This is by design and cannot be bypassed, not even via the timeline.

Assigning screeners

Managers add colleagues under Assigned screeners. Every assignee gets a notification and finds the candidate in My reviews — their personal to-do list, sorted by what still needs a vote:

On the board you can follow the review round without opening a single card: the screener avatars on each card get a green ring as their vote comes in.

With Assistant installed, an optional AI screening hint compares the application against the opening's requirements — labeled "verify yourself", because the humans decide.

06Interviews

No email ping-pong about times: the app reads the interviewers' calendars, and the candidate picks a slot themselves.

Booking an interview

  1. Open the candidate → Interviews tab → Book interview. Give it a title ("Technical interview"), pick the interviewers and a duration.
  2. Decide whether a Talk video room should be created (if Talk is installed). Switched off, you can note a meeting room — or paste a video link from another tool.
  3. Find free slots looks into the interviewers' calendars (working days, 9–17) and proposes times. Untick any you dislike, add slots manually.
  4. Create & write invitation shows the invitation mail — prefilled from your template, with the personal scheduling link. Edit freely and send. (If the link gets lost while editing, the app quietly re-attaches it.)

What the candidate sees

The moment the candidate confirms a slot, everything happens at once: the calendar event lands in all interviewers' calendars, the Talk room is created (for video interviews), the card moves to Interview, and the panel is notified.

If none of the times fit, the candidate clicks None of these times work for me and can add a short message; after confirming, I need to reschedule does the same. Either way the organizer and the interviewers get a notification and the message lands on the candidate's timeline — nothing is cancelled automatically, you decide and propose new times. The day before a confirmed interview (or within the hour, for one booked at shorter notice), the candidate receives a reminder mail with date, time and place (the "Interview reminder" template, chapter 10).

Scheduling links stop working a week after the interview, when the interview is cancelled, and when the candidate is rejected or withdraws — an old link in someone's inbox reveals nothing.

Keeping track

  • Copy scheduling link — if you want to resend it yourself, or the candidate has no email address on file.
  • Cancel interview — notifies all interviewers and removes the calendar event from every interviewer's calendar.
  • My interviews in the navigation lists your own panel appointments, including pending ones.
With Assistant installed, Suggest interview questions drafts questions grounded in the opening's requirements — pick what fits, ignore the rest.

07Offers

Salary data is sensitive — so offers live behind an extra wall, and every offer needs a second pair of eyes before it can go out.

The life of an offer

  1. Draft the terms: job title, salary (amount, currency, per year/month/hour), start date, validity date, internal notes. Only drafts can be edited.
  2. Request approval from a colleague. You can never pick yourself — four eyes are four eyes. The approver is notified, sees exactly this one candidate, and either approves or sends the offer back to draft with a note.
  3. Send the offer email — with the usual preview — or use Mark as sent if the offer went out another way (phone, letter). Both start the validity clock. The card moves to the green Offer column.
  4. Record the answer: Accepted moves the candidate to Hired 🎉 — remember to close the opening and tell the other candidates. Declined keeps them in the pipeline so you decide what happens next. Negotiating marks an ongoing conversation.
Offers expire automatically after their validity date. The creator is warned three days ahead — and the board shows a ⚠️ Stuck chip on the candidate. An expired offer is final; draft a new one if the conversation continues.
Interviewers and observers never see offer terms — not in this tab (it is hidden for them), not on the timeline, not in the activity stream. Mails about offers show up for them only as "an email was sent".

08Rejecting & the talent pool

Rejections decide your reputation as an employer. The app makes the respectful path the easy path.

The rejection flow

  1. Click Reject … — the red button at the bottom of the profile tab, or in the card's ⋮ menu on the board.
  2. Pick an internal reason (not qualified, better candidate chosen, position filled, withdrawn, other). Candidates never see it; your reports do.
  3. Review the rejection mail, prefilled from your template. Edit every word if you like — or reject without an email (for candidates without an address, or when you called them personally).
  4. Optionally tick "Ask to stay in our talent pool".

Rejecting several candidates at once

In the table view, tick the candidates and choose Reject … in the bulk bar. You pick one reason and one mail text; placeholders such as {{candidate_name}} are filled in for each candidate, so everybody still gets a personal mail. Large selections are sent in portions with a progress bar, and the summary at the end lists who was rejected and who could not be (for example because their mail could not be sent) — one problem never stops the others.

Closing an opening

When a position is filled, choose ⋯ → Close opening. If candidates are still active, the app asks what should happen to them: Reject the remaining candidates opens the bulk rejection with the reason "position filled" already chosen, Close without changing them leaves them as they are. Either way, nobody is forgotten: candidates left behind in a closed opening are anonymized automatically after the retention period (chapter 12).

A mistyped opening that never received an application can be removed completely with ⋯ → Delete opening (recruiters only). As soon as an opening has a single candidate, it can only be closed — the history belongs to the candidates.

The talent pool — strictly on consent

The pool invitation adds a consent link to the rejection mail. Only when the candidate clicks it and confirms do they appear in the pool — nobody is kept "just in case" (that's not just polite, it's the GDPR). Pool membership expires automatically after the configured period.

Looking for a particular skill? The search box finds pool members by name, email address or the text of their CV — type "Kubernetes" or "Figma" and see who mentioned it.

When a fitting position opens later, Add to opening creates a fresh copy of the application — documents included — in that opening's "New" column. The pool entry stays until it expires or the person asks to be removed.

09Staying on top of things

The app watches your pipeline so you don't have to.

The Stuck lens

Click in the board header and only candidates where something is stuck remain. A candidate counts as stuck when:

SituationAfter
Waiting in New untouched7 days
In Screening without a single vote5 days
In Interview with no upcoming interview booked3 days
In Offer without an active offer3 days
A sent offer about to expire3 days before expiry
Sitting in any stage30 days

Each stuck card carries the ⚠️ chip — hover it to read exactly what's missing. And independent of the chip, the time-in-stage on every card fades from gray to amber (two weeks) to red (a month).

Notifications

You get a Nextcloud notification (bell, top right) when: a new application arrives in your opening · you are asked to review someone · you are added to an interview panel · a candidate confirms, asks for other times or replies · a candidate withdraws or adds documents on their own page · an offer waits for your approval · an offer is about to expire · someone @mentions you. Recruiters are also told when a candidate asks for their data to be deleted, and when an opening reaches its daily limit of automatic confirmations (a sign of a spam wave). Colleagues who referred someone hear when their referral moves on.

The email digest

Prefer email? Enable the digest — new applications in your openings, pending reviews, upcoming interviews and offers awaiting your approval, in one mail. It is only sent when there is actually something to report.

Recruiting results also show up in Nextcloud's universal search (Ctrl+K / ⌘+K) — type a candidate's name from anywhere in Nextcloud. Words of three letters or more also find candidates whose CV contains them. You only ever find candidates you are allowed to see.

10Mail templates

Five mail types — confirmation of receipt, interview invitation, interview reminder, rejection, offer — each with a built-in template you can replace with your own voice.

In the editor, placeholders like {{candidate_name}} or {{interview_link}} appear as chips — click one to insert it at the cursor. Each mail type only offers the placeholders it can actually fill, and templates are validated when you save: a typo in a placeholder can never reach a candidate.

{{portal_link}} inserts the link to the candidate's own page (chapter 14). The built-in confirmation of receipt contains it; if you use your own template, add it there so applicants can follow their application. The interview reminder knows {{interview_date}}, {{interview_time}} and {{interview_location}} — the Talk link for video interviews.

Names in outgoing mail are cleaned up automatically: line breaks, web addresses and email addresses are removed from {{candidate_name}}, so nobody can misuse the application form to send spam in your company's name. The stored name itself is not changed.
Templates are the starting point, not the final word: every send shows the resolved text first, and you can edit it per candidate.

11Reports

Honest numbers, reconstructed from what actually happened — you only see openings you have access to.

  • The period: Applications from … until … — by default the last twelve months. Every number on the page describes the applications received in that period, so the figures always fit together.
  • Headline tiles: candidates (with this month's trend), hired, average days to hire, open offers.
  • Applications over time — per week for periods up to half a year, per month for longer ones.
  • Stage conversion — how many applications reached at least Screening, Interview, Offer and Hired. Where does your funnel narrow?
  • Source of applications — mail, application form, referral, added manually or from the talent pool. Which channel brings people?
  • Per opening: the funnel in stage colors, the average time spent in each stage, days-to-hire, open offers, sources and the breakdown of rejection reasons.
  • Export CSV downloads the per-opening table for your own spreadsheets.

The numbers are calculated by the database and kept for a few minutes, so the page stays fast even with years of history; any change to a candidate refreshes them.

Numbers survive anonymization: when a rejected candidate's personal data is automatically erased after the retention period, their anonymous journey still counts in the funnel and averages. Your statistics stay honest without keeping personal data.

12Privacy & GDPR

Everything stays on your own server, and the compliance chores run on autopilot.

Where candidate data lives

  • All data is stored in your Nextcloud — nothing is sent to a third party. The one exception is the optional AI assistance: if your admin connected an external AI provider, application texts and CVs go there for the summary — but never the candidate's email address or phone number.
  • Documents live in protected app storage: they never appear in the Files app, and only people with access to that candidate can open them.
  • Access follows the roles from chapter 1 — interviewers only see their assigned candidates and never any salary data.

Automatic retention

Rejected and withdrawn candidates are anonymized automatically after the retention period your admin configures (typically a few months). Name, contact data, documents, mail texts, comments and summaries are erased; the anonymous stage history stays for the reports. The same happens to

  • candidates who were still in the pipeline when their opening was closed — counted from the day of closing,
  • expired talent-pool memberships.

Mails waiting in the triage inbox that nobody assigned to an opening are deleted after their own period (90 days unless your admin changed it) — that is where spam and stray mail end up.

Anonymizing or deleting a candidate also removes their name from the activity stream and from notifications, and rewrites past interview appointments in the interviewers' calendars to a neutral "Interview: <opening>". Their links (scheduling page, own page) stop working.

When a candidate asks…

RequestWhat you do
"What data do you have about me?" Candidate header menu → Export data (GDPR). You get a ZIP with their profile, correspondence, interviews, offer terms and documents. Reviewer names and internal team comments are not included — those are your colleagues' personal data.
"Please delete my data." Candidate header menu → Delete permanently (GDPR). Removes the candidate with all documents, votes, comments, interviews and notifications. Irreversible, with a confirmation dialog. Candidates can also ask on their own page (chapter 14): you then get a notification, the profile shows a red banner with the date of the request, and Delete now does the rest.
"Keep me in mind for later." That's the talent pool — and it only happens after the candidate confirms the consent link themselves (chapter 8).

For your admin

The administration settings (Administration → Recruiting) hold the technical setup: the application mailbox (IMAP), the HR group, the sender identity, the daily limit of automatic confirmations per opening, the retention period, the triage-inbox retention, the talent-pool duration and whether employee referrals are switched on. Admins configure — they don't see candidates.

13Referrals

The best hires often come from people your colleagues already know. Refer someone takes thirty seconds — and every colleague can do it.

  1. Open Refer someone in the navigation. You don't need to be on a hiring team; every Nextcloud user can refer.
  2. Pick one of the open positions, enter the person's name and email address (phone is optional) and write why you recommend them. Attach their CV if you have it.
  3. Tick This person agreed to be referred — ask them first; their data should not end up in a hiring process without their knowledge.
  4. Send referral. The candidate arrives in the opening's "New" column with the source "Employee referral", your note on the timeline, and "Referred by" with your name in the profile. The hiring managers are notified as for any new application.

What you see afterwards

Under Your referrals you follow each referral with a simple status: Received, In review, Interview scheduled, Offer or Closed. When the status changes, you get a notification.

Referring someone gives you no access to their candidate file. You don't see votes, comments, interview feedback or the reason why an application was closed — just the status above. Your admin can switch referrals off in the administration settings.

14The candidate's own page

Applicants shouldn't have to email you to ask how things are going. Every candidate has a personal page that answers that — and lets them act themselves.

How candidates get there

The link is in the confirmation of receipt (and in the interview reminder). It is personal and needs no account. If you write your own templates, add the {{portal_link}} placeholder (chapter 10).

What they see

  • A simple status: received, in review, interview scheduled, offer, or closed. Never your internal stages, votes, comments or rejection reasons — and "closed" only once a decision is final.
  • Their interviews, with the link to pick a time or to see the confirmed appointment.

What they can do

  • Add documents — an updated CV or a certificate. It appears in the profile and the hiring managers are notified.
  • Withdraw the application. The candidate moves to Withdrawn and the managers are notified; booked interviews stay until you cancel them.
  • Ask for their data to be deleted. The recruiters are notified and see the request in the profile (chapter 12); the deletion itself stays a recruiter's decision.
The page stops working when the candidate's data is anonymized or deleted. Until then, only the person holding the link can open it.
Questions this handbook doesn't answer? Ask your recruiting team, or open the candidate's Log tab — chances are the answer is already in the history. Happy hiring! 🎉
